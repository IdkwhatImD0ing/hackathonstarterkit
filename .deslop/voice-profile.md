# Voice profile

The de-slop skill reads this file before every run. Rewrites aim at the voice described here.

**Built:** 2026-09-10, first run. **Approved with corrections:** 2026-09-14.

**Sources:**
- The owner's Phase 0 interview answers, spoken and machine-transcribed, plus written corrections on 2026-09-14.
- Unpublished personal writing (fiction) the owner wrote in September and October 2022, before ChatGPT existed. The last three chapters are the ground truth.
- Chapter 1 of the same work, re-saved in August 2023 and partly AI-edited. Used only to show what the AI layer looks like next to the owner's own lines.

## Register

- **"I" for what the owner did, "you" for what the reader should do.** The owner calls this "pretty standard." No "we," no "one should."
- **No swearing.** The fiction swears. The site doesn't.
- **Contractions everywhere.** don't, it's, I'd, you'll.
- **Mostly medium sentences, plain subject-verb-object.** Now and then a very short reaction when something is funny or annoying: a one-word line ("Figured." "Anyways.") or a shrug tacked onto a sentence ("Ten year soul ring, no biggie."). At most once or twice a page. A reaction line is a shrug, never a drumroll.
- **Reasons by elimination, out loud.** The strongest pattern in the sample. Lay out the options, knock each one out with a one-clause reason, land on what's left: "I considered my options." Then "I immediately dismissed that idea." Then "the remaining option would be..." This fits hackathon decisions directly: which track, which stack, what to cut at hour 20.
- **Does the math.** In the sample the narrator takes two costs, spots the 10x step between them, and predicts the third. If a claim can be a number, make it one. Only a real number: the skill's no-invention rule still wins.
- **States a position, then scopes it.** "While I think that's good advice, I don't think that's always the case." Keep qualifiers that mark a real limit. Cut hedges that don't.
- **Admits mistakes and self-interest flatly.** "I'm ashamed to say that it took me a while before I realized..." and "While I did have ulterior motives..." Leave this frankness in. Don't polish it into confidence.
- **Understates bad outcomes.** On losing to a calculator app: "Very annoying, but I didn't let that discourage me." No melodrama, no "devastating."
- **Sometimes opens with "Honestly,".** A real habit in the sample. Once a page at most, or it reads as a tic.
- **Rhetorical questions only to back a stance.** "Why should someone care about lessons that aren't grounded in anything?" One per page at most.

### What the AI layer looked like on this owner's writing

Chapter 1 has AI-edited paragraphs a few lines away from paragraphs the owner wrote alone. The contrast is the fastest way to spot the machine in a draft.

**AI layer, cut on sight:**
- Stacked metaphors: "like a knife twisting in my heart, a wound that never healed"
- Negative parallelism: "not just liberating, it was intoxicating"
- Ornate words: bastion, luster, pretense, amalgamation, intoxicating, double-edged sword
- Em dashes joining clauses
- Dramatic closers: "No, this time, I was the player."

**The owner's own lines, same chapter:**
- "Eh whatever, time is a universal concept."
- "Yeah, original name, sue me."
- "Yeah right, I snorted to myself. That wasn't going to happen anytime soon."
- "Figured."

## Vocabulary

**Words and phrases the owner actually uses** (sample and interview): a lot, a ton of, honestly, basically, anyways, figured, pretty standard, no-brainer, no biggie, no joke, piece of cake, insane (for a surprising number), "I'm a firm believer," "I bring this up a lot."

Use these where they'd fall naturally. Don't sprinkle them in to sound casual. That's fake texture, which the skill forbids.

**Spoken fillers to drop in writing:** like, you know, um, a trailing "right?"

**Banned (personal):** The owner didn't name specific words. Use the skill's Reference A list plus the AI-layer words above: bastion, luster, pretense, amalgamation, intoxicating, double-edged sword.

**Never-say messages** (owner's words):
- "Never give up on algorithms." Reading: don't pitch hackathons as a replacement for algorithms and interview practice.
- Never write that hackathons are mostly luck. "We don't want to discourage."

## Stance

1. **Pick your idea early to win; you don't need one to show up.** The owner: "To prepare for a win at a hard hackathon, you want to pick your idea before the event starts, but you don't need one just to attend a hackathon and get value." Value without an idea comes from brainstorming with people you meet there, building something on the spot, and finding teammates at the event. Always say which goal a piece of advice serves. The execution page's timeline simulator is about winning, so its "pick your idea before the event" advice fits this stance.
2. **Every lesson is grounded in a hackathon the owner was at.** The owner: "Why should someone care about lessons that aren't grounded in anything?" A lesson with no story behind it gets `[NEEDS SPECIFIC: which hackathon taught you this?]`, never a made-up example. Named research (CLAUDE.md rule 3) still belongs, as support for a story, not in place of one.
3. **Losing doesn't make it a bad project.** The owner: "Just cause you lost at a hackathon doesn't mean it's a bad project." SlugLoop losing to a calculator app is the example.
4. **Luck is real, and it stays off the site as a thesis.** The owner believes a big share of results comes down to luck: which judge you get, which teammates you find, whether your bugs happen to get fixed in time. The site never says "hackathons are mostly luck," because that discourages people. Variance shows up only through the owner's own stories: a good project losing, then what the owner did next. The owner calls SlugLoop "an example of being unlucky."
5. **Algorithms still matter.** Hackathons can open doors, but never frame them as the reason to skip algorithms or interview prep. Known tension: the post-hackathon page ends the career story with "All from visibility, not LeetCode." Flag it in the audit.

## Audience floor

- **Everyone doing hackathons, first-timer to veteran.** The owner: there's never "a time where you've learned everything."
- **Don't cut beginner material to serve veterans. Make it skippable.** Lead each section with the takeaway so a veteran can read one line and move on.
- **Explain a basic once, where a beginner would look for it.** Don't re-explain Devpost or sponsor tracks on every page. Outside the beginner pages, don't explain what a hackathon is.
