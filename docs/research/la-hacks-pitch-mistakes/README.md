# LA Hacks Pitch Mistakes Research

## Purpose

This folder supports the blog post `9 Hackathon Pitch Mistakes I Saw at LA Hacks`.
The article is based on first-hand LA Hacks judging observations and backed by
presentation, hackathon, and pitch deck sources.

## Article Thesis

Most hackathon pitch mistakes are not about weak code. They are about making the
judge work too hard: too much generic context, too many speakers, too many
slides, too many side features, and too little time for follow-up questions.

The stronger pattern is:

1. Read the judge.
2. Put the clearest speaker in charge.
3. Bring energy.
4. Use slides only to frame the problem.
5. Skip concepts the judge already knows.
6. Demo the main user flow.
7. Have a backup video.
8. Keep an appendix for deeper questions.
9. Protect Q&A time.

## Research Themes

### Audience Fit

Audience analysis sources support the idea that presenters should adjust
language, examples, and depth based on what the audience already knows and cares
about. This directly supports the points about judge role and not explaining
tools the judge already understands.

### Delivery

Group presentation sources support choosing a confident lead speaker and keeping
one coherent message. Emotional contagion and smile mimicry research supports
the practical point that judges respond to the team's energy.

### Slides and Cognitive Load

Presentation design sources support short, visual slides and warn against
duplicating spoken content as dense on-screen text. This backs the argument that
paragraph-heavy slides make judges choose between reading and listening.

### Demo and Submission Materials

Devpost sources support recording a clear demo video, uploading it correctly,
and treating it as a judging asset. These sources are especially useful for the
LA Hacks Wi-Fi anecdote and the deliberation point.

### Q&A and Appendix

Pitch appendix sources support keeping the main story simple while storing
deeper evidence for follow-up questions. Pitch competition sources support
explicit Q&A time and preparing answers before judging.

## How to Use This Research in the Blog

- Keep the post first-person and practical. The strongest material is the LA

- Use research as reinforcement, not as the main voice of the post.
- Avoid over-citing inside the rendered blog unless the format supports links.
